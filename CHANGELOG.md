# Changelog

## 0.7.67 - 2026-10-07

An opt-in temperature-anchored load predictor, temperature strategies in Strategy Comparison, and a sensor check for the prediction config. This version also ships the CI, tooling and license-notice changes that were under Unreleased (below). **Nothing changes in the live load forecast, the LP, the DESS mapping or any Victron/MQTT write unless someone selects the Temperature predictor type**, except for the review follow-ups below (rebalancing, the PV-curtailment restore and the Home Assistant price feed). With `activeType: 'historical'` (as in production) the load forecast makes no Open-Meteo call, and saving the form leaves `temperaturePredictor` out of `prediction-config.json` until it is used. The only automatic change is that Run Comparison now also makes one Open-Meteo call and scores the extra temperature rows.

- **Temperature-anchored load predictor (opt-in)** (from upstream #191/#193: d55cb7c, 30823e8, e3e4f23, 463333b, c33840a and the final e318148/eb4f98c form of `lib/load-predictor-temperature.ts`).
  - **What it does.** Each past day is reduced to an effective temperature: a 4:2:1 blend of its own mean outside temperature and the two days before it. Per day bucket, the days are sorted by that temperature and cut into quantile bins. Each bin becomes an anchor: its median temperature plus a median load profile per hour. A forecast hour interpolates between the two anchors either side of the day's effective temperature. Beyond the outermost anchors it extrapolates, clamped to half the anchor span and floored at the lowest value anywhere in the anchors (the idle draw). Temperatures are Open-Meteo `temperature_2m` (default model) at the PV site coordinates. The Forecast API accepts `past_days` up to 92 but returns null for hours older than about 68 days (a probe on 2026-10-06 got 576 null hours, the first non-null one 68 days back), so every temperature window is sized to stay inside 68 days. Nothing is fitted or stored; the anchors are rebuilt on every run.
  - **Fork changes.**
    - **Local days and hours.** Days and hours are keyed on the local calendar instead of UTC, the same way the historical predictor walks local days. With UTC keys every profile shifts an hour after a DST switch (next one: 25 Oct), which would bias the comparison exactly when heating-season data starts. The repeated hour of a fall-back day is averaged, not summed. A day needs at least 20 hourly temperatures before its mean counts.
    - **One active predictor, not upstream's summed list (#190).** It is a third `activeType`, `'temperature'`, with `temperaturePredictor: { sensor, lookbackWeeks 1–8, dayFilter, bins 2–8 }`. The schema validates it on save and clamps it on load. The 8-week cap keeps the live fetch (lookback + recent-accuracy week + margins) at 66 `past_days`, inside the ~68 days Open-Meteo fills; upstream's 12 weeks needs 93, and anything from 9 weeks up would quietly build its anchors from fewer days than configured. Lookback days that still come back without a temperature add a `warnings` entry ("N of M days in the temperature lookback … have no Open-Meteo temperature"); the forecast is then built from the days that remain. The sensor is the form's shared Sensor select.
    - **The historical predictor is a mandatory fallback.** If there are no coordinates, Open-Meteo fails, or no forecast hour has an anchor, the whole forecast comes from `historicalPredictor`. Hours without a temperature prediction are filled from it, never with 0 W. Each fallback is logged and returned as `warnings`, and the Predictions tab shows it in amber ("Load forecast updated: …"). Only without a historical predictor does the forecast throw, which keeps the stored series like any other failed forecast. `executeLoadForecast` therefore requires both predictors for this type. An HA failure throws exactly as on the historical path. Without coordinates the fallback starts before any HA query, and when Open-Meteo fails the history already fetched is reused for the fallback whenever it covers it (same sensor, historical lookback no longer than the temperature one), so a persistent fallback does not double the recorder load every auto-calculate tick.
    - **Recent accuracy is out of sample.** Anchors are rebuilt for each scored day with the cutoff at that day's start (c33840a).
    - **Error mapping.** Open-Meteo errors now map to 502 on every prediction path, not only PV, and are checked before the HA "timed out" rule.
  - **How to enable it.** In Predictions → Load Forecast, set Predictor Type to "Temperature (opt-in)", or press Use on a temperature row in Strategy Comparison. Use on a temperature row also moves the historical fallback's sensor to the row's sensor; the rest of the stored historical strategy is kept as it is on the server, so a tab left open across an auto-select run does not write its stale strategy back. Select Historical to switch back. README.md (also the add-on's Documentation tab) describes the same steps and the API fields. The strategy auto-select never selects it. While it is active, the daily selection records a skip ("active predictor is "temperature", not historical") and leaves `historicalPredictor`, the fallback, untouched.
- **Strategy Comparison scores temperature strategies too** (upstream 82883e5 + c33840a, adapted to the fork's scoring core).
  - **The grid.** Per sensor it adds 64 rows: lookback 2/4/6/8 weeks × 4 day filters × 2/3/4/6 bins. Each scored day is predicted from anchors built only from the days before it. Temperatures come from one Open-Meteo request (67 `past_days` for the 7-day window, inside the ~68 days with data); missing days add a warning.
  - **Same hours as the historical rows.** A temperature row is scored on the hours its sensor's historical rows share, intersected with the hours it could predict itself. The historical rows and their numbers are unchanged, and a temperature row that covers every hour has the same `n` as the rows it is ranked against.
  - **Table and buttons.** The table gains a Type column, and Params shows the aggregation or the bin count. Chart works for temperature rows: `POST /predictions/validate/strategy` with `type: 'temperature'` is served from the last run's cached history and temperatures. Use switches the live predictor, as above.
  - **When it is skipped.** Without coordinates, or when Open-Meteo fails, the temperature rows are left out and the status line says why, in amber: the comparison itself succeeded, so a site that never opted in no longer sees a red status on every run. The historical rows still stand.
  - **Cost.** The grid is scored one sensor at a time, yielding to the event loop in between. On the dev box it adds about 0.5 s for 10 sensors.
  - **How to read it.** The backtest uses observed temperatures where live use has a forecast, so temperature rows look somewhat better than they will perform live. The window is 7 days. Production has no heat-pump sub-meter, so expect a gain, if any, only once heating-season days are in the lookback.
- **Prediction sensors are checked against Home Assistant** (upstream a954991 + 0e05b6b, redone for the fork's JSON editor, server-side).
  - **The endpoint.** `POST /predictions/sensors/check` takes the editors' unsaved `sensors`/`derived` (or the stored ones) and makes one `GET /api/states` with a 10 s deadline. `fetchHaEntityStates` gained an optional `timeoutMs`; other callers are unchanged.
  - **Entity checks.** Each entity reports: not found (a typo or renamed id would silently contribute no data), a non-numeric state, a unit that is not Wh/kWh/MWh, a configured unit that differs from HA's (with the scale error, e.g. ×1000), and a `state_class` other than total/total_increasing.
  - **Formula checks.** Each derived formula is checked for terms that match no sensor name, or that use a derived series before it is defined. Both would count as 0.
  - **Advisory only.** An unreachable HA comes back as `reachable: false` with a 200, and the UI shows "Saving is not affected". The check never blocks or undoes a save.
  - **In the UI.** The Settings → Sensors card has a "Check sensors in Home Assistant" button and a per-entity status list. The check also runs when either editor's change is committed, but not on page load (each check is a full `GET /api/states`, and a standalone install without HA would see an "unreachable" warning on every visit). An edit drops a check still in flight (the 0e05b6b sequence guard).

### Review follow-ups

These change live behaviour only where noted: rebalancing (when `rebalanceEnabled` is on), the PV-curtailment restore, and how the Home Assistant price feed is read. With rebalancing off the plan, the DESS mapping and `data.json` are unchanged.

- **A rebalance hold now starts on a pack that tops out at 99 %.** The hold clock used to start only once live SoC read at least `maxSoc_percent`. Production packs peak at 99 % system SoC, so with rebalancing on the clock never started, and every plan kept the battery held at the target with battery→grid export blocked, indefinitely. The clock now starts when live SoC is within 1 percentage point of the rebalance target (`REBALANCE_START_TOLERANCE_PERCENT`) and the plan holds from slot 0, as before.
- **A rebalance hold that never starts is given up after 3 days.** The first plan with rebalancing on and no hold started records `rebalanceState.pendingSinceMs`. If the hold has still not started 3 days later (`REBALANCE_PENDING_GIVE_UP_MS`), that plan maps no hold window, and rebalancing switches itself off with a warning, like a completed cycle. Export can no longer stay blocked forever. Switching rebalancing off drops the marker, so turning it on again starts a fresh 3 days.
- **The hold clock is only started by an Optimal plan.** A time-limited plan is display-only (never written to Victron), but it could still stamp the hold start.
- **The pinned-hold solve now matches its documentation.**
  - A pinned solve that stops at the time limit, even with a usable incumbent, now frees the hold window and re-solves, as 0.7.65 described. Before, the pinned incumbent was returned.
  - When the hold covers the whole horizon, an infeasible pin no longer solves the identical free LP a second time.
  - When the relaxation search frees the window because it ran out of budget or time, the warning says "search stopped (budget/time limit)". It no longer says that no start cap exists.
- **PV curtailment restores are retried in more cases.**
  - An unreadable PV-disable record with no `portalId` configured, at a boot where the GX serial cannot be read, is now kept pending and retried. The serial is detected on each retry, and the status shows `restorePending`. Before, the restore was dropped until the next restart, with no warning in the status.
  - A stop or restart during a disable's serial lookup now records nothing. A stop or restart while the disable's record is being saved now picks up the restore, also when the feature was restarted switched off. Before, the record could stay pending with nothing retrying it.
  - A read-back that answers `Pv/Disable=1` resets the "never answered" count. The 10-minute give-up therefore only applies to read-backs that never answer, as documented. Its time span is now measured on a monotonic clock, so a wall-clock jump at boot cannot trigger an instant give-up.
- **Home Assistant price feed.**
  - A point at the same instant as the previous one is now skipped, and the first one is kept. Before, it ended the whole feed. A tomorrow list that repeats today's 23:00 no longer cuts the window. A naive feed that stamps both 02:00 hours of the autumn DST day with the summer offset is now repaired like a merged hour.
  - A clean feed whose prices all lie in the past (a stale sensor) now keeps the stored prices instead of overwriting them. The defective-feed path already did this.

### CI, dev tooling and license notices (previously Unreleased)

**None of these change the add-on at runtime.** The image keeps the same Node, the same launcher and the same production `node_modules`: the runtime dependency tree in the lockfile is unchanged, and a local amd64 build had file-identical `node_modules` apart from an empty `@esbuild/` directory that `npm prune` leaves behind. The license files ship in the image with this version (the Builder only rebuilds when `optivolt/` changes, and this bump changes `optivolt/config.yaml`).

- **CI boots the add-on image and solves a plan in it** (idea from upstream 3675084, rewritten for the fork). A new `docker-smoke` job in the Tests workflow runs on every push and PR. It stages `api/ app/ lib/ vendor/highs-build package*.json` into `optivolt/` exactly as `build-addon.yaml` does, builds `optivolt/Dockerfile` for amd64 and starts it without a Supervisor. It polls `/health` for up to 60 s, switches `dataSources.soc` to `api` (the default `mqtt` would try to read a Victron) and `POST`s `/calculate` with no data refresh and no Victron write. The job fails unless the plan is Optimal with at least one slot, and prints the container logs when any step fails. It has a 20-minute timeout, so a stalled base-image pull or `npm ci` cannot hold a runner for GitHub's 6-hour default. Before, the Builder built the image only when `optivolt/` changed and never started it, so a lockfile, `api/` or `lib/` change that broke the boot would only have surfaced on the production add-on. A unit test keeps the smoke job's staging step identical to the Builder's.
- **CI hardening.** The Tests workflow now runs with `permissions: contents: read` and checks out with `persist-credentials: false`. Before, a push to main ran `npm ci` install scripts and the Codecov action with the repo's default write token stored in the git config. The `home-assistant/actions` helpers (`info`, `find-addons`) are pinned to commit `06749dd` (master as of 2026-09-27) instead of following `@master`; Home Assistant documents them as internal and subject to change without warning. The pin comment says only `# master`, because Dependabot would move the SHA but leave a date in the comment stale.
- **Dependabot also watches GitHub Actions**, as one grouped monthly PR. This includes the helper SHA pins. A `docker` entry is deliberately left out, because Dependabot cannot follow the `ARG BUILD_FROM` base and would only propose Node-major bumps of the `node:24-alpine` deps stage.
- **License notices for vendored code** (texts from upstream aaa5bf6). `app/vendor/licenses/` now holds the MIT texts for Chart.js, patternomaly and Tailwind and the OFL-1.1 texts for Outfit and JetBrains Mono. `app/vendor/README.md` indexes them and records the versions: Chart.js 4.5.1, patternomaly 1.3.2, Tailwind 4.3.3, Outfit v15 and JetBrains Mono v24 (non-Mac variants). `vendor/highs-build/LICENSE` is the MIT text of highs-js and HiGHS, copied verbatim from the `highs@1.15.3` tarball, and the PROVENANCE refresh procedure now copies it with the binaries. Tests check that every vendored file has a notice.
- **Dev tooling.**
  - `scripts/write-target-soc.ts` is deleted. Nothing referenced it, and it wrote `TargetSoc = 100` to all 48 live DESS slots.
  - `tsx` is now an exact devDependency (4.23.15). The solver-gate scripts and docs call it with `npx --no-install tsx`. Before, `npx tsx` ran whatever version the machine's npx cache held, or fetched the latest; on the dev box that was 4.21.0. The broken `#!/usr/bin/env npx tsx` shebang is now `#!/usr/bin/env -S npx --no-install tsx`.
  - `tsconfig.json` enables `erasableSyntaxOnly` and `verbatimModuleSyntax`. These reject syntax that Node's type stripping cannot run, which matters because the add-on runs `node api/index.ts`. `tsconfig.json` also typechecks `scripts/`. No code changes were needed.
- **Dev dependencies.** vitest and @vitest/coverage-v8 go to 5.0.3, eslint to 10.12.0, globals to 17.13.0, jsdom to 30.1.2 and supertest to 7.3.1. npm `overrides` clear the dev-only advisories without a downgrade:
  - nodemon's `chokidar` → ^4.0.3. This removes `braces`, which has no patched release.
  - `@tailwindcss/cli`'s `@parcel/watcher` → ^2.6.0. That drops `micromatch`; remove this override once a Tailwind release ships with 2.6.
  - `micromark-extension-math`'s `katex` → ^0.18.11 (GHSA-238p-pmpm-9mq7, published 2026-10-05). It is only reached through `@eslint/markdown`.

  The lockfile refresh also takes `brace-expansion` to 5.0.12 and `source-map-js` to 1.2.2. `npm audit` now reports 0. `npm run build:css` output is byte-identical. chokidar 4 no longer understands the glob ignores nodemon passes it (`**/node_modules/**`, `./data/**/*`), so with the old `npm run dev` command nodemon would have walked and watched all of `node_modules/` (about 9,350 files instead of a few hundred). `npm run dev` therefore now watches only `api/` and `lib/` (`--watch api --watch lib --ext ts,json`, 88 files) and no longer needs `--ignore 'data/'`. It restarts on `api/` and `lib/` changes and ignores writes under `data/`. Edits to `vendor/` or `package.json` no longer restart it.

## 0.7.66 - 2026-10-06

Persistence, settings and UI hygiene. **Nothing changes in the LP, the DESS mapping or any Victron/MQTT write** for valid settings and a located PV site (as in production). What changes: some bad `POST /settings` values now get a 400 instead of being stored, and a few UI panels draw differently.

- **JSON stores are durable across power loss** (from upstream 62d2dcf, adapted). `writeJson` now writes the temp file through a file handle, fsyncs it, renames it over the target and then fsyncs the directory (best effort). Before, a power cut after the rename could leave a zero-length `settings.json` or `data.json`. Both stores rethrow on such a file at load, which stops planning and DESS writes until someone repairs it by hand. The `<file>.<pid>.<n>.tmp` naming, the per-path lock and the boot sweep are unchanged. A failed write or fsync (for example ENOSPC) now also removes its temp file; before, only a failed rename did.
- **`POST /vrm/refresh-settings` no longer returns the stored HA token** (from upstream ed1337b). It redacts the token like `GET`/`POST /settings` (omits `haToken`, adds `hasHaToken`). It also reports `isAddon`, so a VRM refresh no longer shows the HA URL/token fields inside the add-on.
- **The VRM refresh keeps persisted fields it does not know about.** This is a regression test only: the rebuild already spreads the stored file since 0.7.64 (`updateData`). Upstream lost a field (`evLastState`) to the old hand-kept key list.
- **Opening the Predictions tab writes nothing** (from upstream c161795, adapted).
  - **What was wrong.** On first open the tab POSTed the whole prediction form and then ran a persisting forecast. If the config GET had just failed (an ingress or network blip), the form still held its HTML defaults, and that POST silently replaced the stored PV location and predictor (lat/lon 0,0, clear-sky, hourly, historical). Production uses `pv: 'api'`, so every later plan would have run on a PV forecast for the wrong place.
  - **The fix.** The tab now runs a read-only preview on open: `POST /predictions/forecast?persist=false` returns the forecasts and accuracy data without writing `data.json` or pruning adjustments. That keeps the accuracy charts the fork shows on open. The form records whether it hydrated from the server. Until it has, every save path (debounced field edits, Run, Apply suggestion, the comparison table's Run and Use) refuses with "Prediction settings were not loaded from the server; reload the page before saving".
- **Blank PV coordinates are null, never (0, 0)** (form part of upstream 463333b; the server guard is fork work).
  - **Form.** A cleared latitude or longitude field now reads as `null`. The schema still rejects null, so the save fails visibly ("Save failed: pvConfig.latitude must be a finite number") and the stored location stays.
  - **Server.** A shared `hasPvCoordinates` treats missing, non-finite and the exact (0, 0) pair as unset. The manual forecast skips PV; `runPvForecast` throws; the VRM refresh logs `PV forecast skipped` and keeps the stored PV series without retrying.
- **Settings saves reject bad values instead of storing them.** `POST /settings` now validates the incoming patch before merging, with a 400 that names the field.
  - **Efficiencies.** Charge, discharge, inverter and EV charge efficiency must be numbers from 1 to 100. A discharge efficiency of 0 (a cleared field sends 0) made `buildLP` emit literal `Infinity` coefficients, so every solve failed until someone fixed the setting. There is deliberately no clamp: a value already on disk is loaded unchanged and keeps failing the solve loudly, because a silent 1 % clamp would write a nonsense schedule every cycle. A null EV efficiency still falls back to its default, as before. The inputs get `min="1" max="100"`. In the UI a rejected save stays on the status line: a Run (or an auto-solving field edit) still solves on the stored settings, and the status reads "Settings not saved: <message>. Plan updated (using the previously saved settings)" in red instead of a green "Plan updated". A standalone save failure ("Settings error: …") is now red too.
  - **Booleans and enums** (from upstream 22374cd, reject-on-save only). The top-level flags (`rebalanceEnabled`, `blockFeedInOnNegativePrices`, `evEnabled` and the EV planning/actuation toggles) must be JSON booleans. `terminalSocValuation`, `evDepartureDay`, `evFailSafeMode` and `evOverrideMode` must be one of their values. Before, `rebalanceEnabled: "off"` was stored and is truthy, so it forced a charge to maxSoc. The load path is untouched (no throw, no coercion). The shipped defaults and the production settings shape validate unchanged, and the UI always sends real booleans.
- **Charts keep their gridlines when the theme flips** (from upstream d676eb6). `refreshAllChartThemes` recoloured `chart.options`, Chart.js's resolved view, where the x-axis colour callback reads back as a string. It was replaced with one colour, so after any light/dark switch (including the OS flip at dusk) every timeline chart drew a line at every 15-minute slot until the next render. It now recolours `chart.config.options`.
- **Adjustment and last-full-SoC times use a 24-hour clock** (from upstream a2968d5 + db66c12). These were the only two formatters that followed the browser locale, so en-US showed AM/PM. They now set `hourCycle: 'h23'`; the tests pin en-US.
- **The schedule table stays readable on a narrow screen** (from upstream 3658017). Thousands are grouped with U+202F (narrow no-break space) instead of U+2009 (thin space), so values over 999 no longer wrap mid-number and double the row height. The header row wraps as a whole instead of breaking the toggle labels word by word.
- **A panel that fails to draw no longer reports a solved plan as an error** (controller part of upstream 3465784, extended). Each panel (plan info, summary, rebalance notice, schedule table, each overview chart, EV panel) now renders on its own, so one throw no longer stops the rest. Before, any render throw replaced "Plan updated and sent to Victron" with a red "Error" and blanked the summary, even though the server had already solved and written the plan. Now it shows an amber "Plan calculated (and sent to Victron), but display failed: <panel>: <message>". A non-optimal plan keeps its status in front ("Plan status: Time limit reached, but display failed: …"), for a fresh and a cached plan alike. A cached plan whose render fails still counts as loaded, so boot does not start a solve that would hit the same failure.
- **The EV target is read from the plan rows.** `parseSolution` puts the target the LP enforced (`ev_target_soc_percent`: the live HA entity value or the setting) on the row it is pinned to. That is the slot ending at the deadline, or the last slot when no deadline is set. The EV SoC charts and both tables use that row and value when present, and otherwise fall back to the browser-side settings. Production has no deadline set, so the EV tab now shows the 90 % target at the end of the horizon instead of no marker and an all-dash Target column. The "ready" badge is kept when the deadline lies within one step after the end of the pinned row (the server floors the deadline to a slot, so an off-boundary 07:10 badge lands two rows after the pin) and dropped otherwise (an elapsed "today" deadline the server moved to the end of the horizon). Nothing that actuates reads the field.

## 0.7.65 - 2026-10-06

A rebalance hold is now written to Victron as a hold, and a hold that has started can no longer drift later in the horizon. The dead V1 DESS mapper is removed. **Nothing changes while `rebalanceEnabled` is off** (as in production): without a hold window the mapper output and the LP are identical to 0.7.64.

- **Hold-window slots are mapped to a DESS hold** (from upstream 583f704 + 61a045b, adapted).
  - **What was wrong.** The solver's hold window never reached the mapper: `extractRebalanceWindow` ran after `mapRowsToDessV2`. Inside the window the SoC sits at max, so each slot is its own SoC segment and the price logic found no tipping point:
    - PV-surplus slots became **selfConsumption / no restrictions**. Victron drops the target for selfConsumption, so load drained the battery during the hold. In a production-snapshot probe, 4 of 12 window slots got this (12 of 12 with PV ×3).
    - A saturated grid-charge slot ending at 100 % got the +5 % boost clamp `maxSoc − 1` = **99**. That misses Victron's keep-charged path, which needs a target ≥ 100.
  - **The fix.** `computePlan` extracts the window before mapping and passes it as `DessMapperOptions.rebalanceWindow` (inclusive). Window slots become **proBattery / battery→grid blocked (grid→battery allowed) / target = `rebalanceTargetSoc_percent`** (clamped to `maxSoc_percent` like the LP). There is no CV or `maxSoc − 1` clamp. Feed-in keeps its price-based value, so the negative-price block still applies. On the same snapshot all 12 window slots now map to the hold. `lastPlan.rebalanceWindow` is unchanged.
- **A started hold is pinned to slot 0.**
  - **What was wrong.** The LP could place the remaining hold slots anywhere in the horizon while the wall-clock countdown kept running. A probe with a full battery exported at 15 kW, recharged at a cheaper hour and re-entered the hold later; the cycle then auto-completed on the clock after about 15 minutes of real hold. Upstream has the same bug.
  - **The fix.** Once `rebalanceState.startMs` is set (and slots remain), config-builder sets the new `SolverConfig.rebalanceMaxStartSlot = 0`, and `buildLP` caps the window start there (upstream ecc27d5's `KMAX` start cap). Only `start_balance_0` exists, and the SoC forcing covers exactly the first D slots.
  - **When the pin is infeasible, it is relaxed only as far as needed (fork-specific).** If the battery has sagged too far below the target to be back there by the end of slot 0 (e.g. SoC 85 % with the learned 2.8 kW taper above 98 %), the pinned LP is infeasible. Releasing the window outright would bring the drift back: the solver could export now and hold much later while the countdown runs. Instead the planner searches for the smallest start cap k at which the hold is feasible (feasibility only grows with k): it starts from a physical lower bound (deficit ÷ `maxChargePower_W` × charge efficiency per slot), gallops up (k, k+1, k+3, …) and bisects back down. When the gallop overshoots to T − D (which is no cap at all) it solves the free window and still bisects below it, so a feasible cap under T − D is not skipped. Only an Optimal probe counts as feasible; a time-limited incumbent never replaces an Optimal answer, because computePlan would only display such a plan and never write it. The search is bounded to 8 relaxed solves and 20 s, and each relaxed probe's solver time limit is cut to what is left of that budget (at least 1 s). When it runs out it keeps the best cap found, or frees the window if none was found. The window is also freed (the 0.7.64 behaviour) when only T − D is feasible, or when a pinned or gallop solve ends with neither Optimal nor Infeasible (e.g. a time limit), rather than failing the plan and leaving a stale schedule on the GX. Each relaxed cycle logs one warning, and the plan summary carries `rebalanceHoldMaxStartSlot`, the cap that applied. The EV preview skips the pinned solve when the main plan was relaxed, starts its search at the main plan's cap, and does not repeat the warning. Residual: the countdown keeps running while the battery recovers, so a sagged hold delivers less than the requested hold time. Not restarting the clock is deliberate, because a pack that cannot stay at target would otherwise never finish the cycle.
  - **The hold clock starts only on a plan that holds from slot 0.** `rebalanceState.startMs` used to be stamped whenever live SoC was at or above `maxSoc_percent`, wherever the (unpinned) solver had put the window. If the first 100 % reading landed in a high export-price slot, that plan drained the battery to grid while the countdown started; the next cycle was then pinned from a lower SoC and could complete on the clock with no real hold. The stamp now also requires the plan's own window to start at slot 0.
- **The V1 DESS mapper (`mapRowsToDess`) is deleted** (upstream 971c527, redone locally). Only tests used it since b1120fe. It had drifted from V2 (no `g2ev` in its saturation check, no CV cap, an unused `feedinAllowed` local), and its unterminated `/* v8 ignore end */` hid every shared helper and the production `mapRowsToDessV2` from coverage. The tipping-point, empty-rows, flags and max-SoC segment-boundary tests now target V2; the other V1 strategy tests are dropped.

## 0.7.64 - 2026-10-06

The planner now builds the solver config once. Since 2026-04-30 (a848f1b), every production plan (`dataSources.soc = 'mqtt'`) silently dropped the adaptive-learning charge taper, the EV charge taper and the manual prediction adjustments. **This changes live plans: read the deploy gate below before deploying.**

- **Calibration, EV taper and prediction adjustments reach the LP again.**
  - **What was wrong.** With `soc = 'mqtt'`, `computePlan` read the live SoC and then rebuilt the config from raw `data.json`. That threw away what `getSolverInputs` had just applied: the prediction adjustments, `applyCalibration` (learned charge/CV thresholds) and `applyEvCalibration` (learned EV taper). The log still printed `Applying calibration: N charge thresholds` on every cycle, and the UI showed adjustments as applied while the plan ignored them.
  - **Why the 2026-06-18 analysis was affected.** Because of this rebuild, the learned discharge taper blamed in that analysis never reached the LP. The dess-mapper change was the part of that fix that actually worked.

The planner no longer rebuilds anything. The EV preview (car unplugged) uses `buildPlannerConfig` too, so it gets the same calibration and adjustments.
  - **Failure handling.** A failed or empty MQTT read still answers 503 with the same messages, and now writes nothing at all.
- **Rebalance auto-disable happens after the solve** (from upstream 97c203b, adapted). A completed hold cycle (`remainingSlots = 0`) adds no rebalance variables or constraints, so it is now solved with its config as built, calibration included. Previously the config was rebuilt without calibration.
  - `rebalanceEnabled: false` and the cleared `rebalanceState` are written only after the solve, parse and DESS mapping succeed, so a failure in any of them changes nothing.
  - The hold-start stamp follows the same rule.
  - The fork's locked `updateSettings` is kept. Upstream's full-snapshot `saveSettings` is not ported, because it would bring back the clobber fixed in 0.7.56.
- **`data.json` writers patch the file under a lock instead of writing back old snapshots.** `data-store` gains `updateData(mutate)`, a read-modify-write under `withJsonLock` like `updateSettings`; `saveData` now queues behind the same lock.
  - **What was wrong.** The planner wrote back the whole `data.json` it had loaded before the HA EV reads and the MQTT SoC read (several seconds). The VRM/forecast refresh did the same around the forecast and HA-price fetches. A prediction adjustment, `POST /data` or forecast persist that landed in that window was silently reverted on disk.
  - **Who now patches.** These writers now patch only their own fields on the current file:
    - the planner (`soc`, `rebalanceState`, pruned adjustments, `lastFullSocAt`);
    - the series refresh (only the series it actually refreshed, so a failed fetch keeps what is on disk);
    - prediction-adjustment CRUD;
    - forecast persists;
    - `POST /data`.
  - **Not ported.** Upstream's inputs-version guard (ade24f7) is not ported: our solve is synchronous, so the lock is the fix.

Now that the learned taper reaches the LP, a slot capped by the taper also counts as saturated. The cap is the one in force at the slot's start SoC, mirroring `c_charge_cap_t`. ## 0.7.63 - 2026-10-06

Solver-status hardening: a solve that does not finish Optimal no longer replaces the plan the EV charger, PV curtailment, the shore optimizer and adaptive learning act on, and every solve is bounded in time.

- **A solve with no usable solution is an error, not an all-zero plan** (port of upstream 76f61c0 and a29c20c, adapted). `parseSolution` never read the solver status. An infeasible, unbounded or errored solve parsed into rows of zero flows at 0 % SoC, which became `lastPlan` with a fresh timestamp. Only the DESS write was refused (the 0.7.45 guard, whose entry said "the display path is unchanged"; this release reverses that). Meanwhile the EV actuator saw `ev_charge = 0` and could switch off a charging car, PV curtailment saw the full import cap as free headroom, the shore optimizer saw `idle`, and a 0 %-SoC snapshot went into plan history. Now `parseSolution` throws `SolverStatusError` unless the status is `Optimal`, or an early stop (time or iteration limit, objective bound or target) that carries a finite objective and a finite `soc_t` value for every slot. The finite-objective check is fork-specific: the vendored HiGHS 1.15.1 returns a time limit hit before any incumbent as all-zero values with objective `Infinity`, which upstream's check alone lets through. `POST /calculate` answers 502 with the status in the message and in `details.solverStatus`. Auto-calculate, the DESS price refresh and the EV reconcile already catch the error and write nothing, so DESS keeps the last schedule.
- **Only an Optimal solve becomes the active plan** (port of upstream a10005c, extended). An early-stop incumbent (a feasible but unproven plan) is returned to `POST /calculate` for display, with its status shown in amber as before. It is not cached as `lastPlan`, not passed to PV curtailment, gets no EV preview and no plan-history snapshot, and is still refused for the Victron write. Its response carries `evPreview: null` rather than the preview cached with the previous Optimal plan, so the EV tab never shows a preview that belongs to a different plan. The previous Optimal plan stays active for every consumer; the EV actuator stops acting on it once it is older than `evMaxPlanAgeSeconds` (`stale_plan`, no charger write). PV curtailment and the shore optimizer have no plan-age check yet. `GET /calculate/last` also returns 404 for a non-Optimal cached plan, as a second check.
- **Every HiGHS solve has a 30 s time limit** (port of upstream 8cb3a29). The solve runs synchronously on the event loop that also serves HTTP, auto-calculate and the MQTT keepalive, and nothing bounded it. Production ticks take at most about 12 s (EV-plugged solves up to about 9 s on the dev box), so the limit does not change normal planning. A hit with no incumbent is now a `SolverStatusError`; with an incumbent it is display-only. The EV preview uses the same options, so the worst case is two 30 s solves per cycle. The `[calculate] solve` log line now includes `status`. The solver-refresh gate (`scripts/compare-highs-builds.ts`) solves with the limit too and fails with a clear message when either build returns no usable solution or stops short of `Optimal` (two builds that both hit the limit with an incumbent within the MIP gap of each other prove nothing); its objective tolerance still comes from the gap-only `MIP_SOLVE_OPTIONS`.
- **A throwing EV-preview solve resets the shared HiGHS instance.** The main solve already discarded the WASM instance when `highs.solve` threw, since a trap may leave its heap corrupted, but the display-only EV preview (car unplugged, native EV mode) shares that instance and only logged. Now a throw from the preview's `highs.solve` resets it too, so the next solve starts on a fresh instance. A preview whose result has no usable solution just leaves the preview empty and keeps the instance.

## 0.7.62 - 2026-10-06

Time-series hardening ahead of the 2026-10-25 autumn DST change: Home Assistant prices are placed by their timestamps, load and price series that start too late are refused, and two chart aggregations no longer misreport energy.

- **Home Assistant prices are placed by timestamp, so the fall-back day no longer shifts them by an hour.** The GE-Spot hourly-average sensor merges the two 02:00 hours of the 25-hour day into one `+02:00` entry, so 2026-10-25 has 24 entries for 25 hours. The parser concatenated the entries and took the start from the first one. Every price from 03:00 CET on landed one hour early, from the moment Oct 25 prices publish (about 13:00 on Oct 24) until the rollover into Oct 26. Charge, discharge and export timing would have been written to the Multi RS against the wrong hours. The same silent shift happened on any day the sensor drops an hour. Now `fetchPricesFromHA`:
  - accepts only finite numbers or strictly numeric strings as prices. `null`, `''`, booleans and other values used to become 0 c/kWh (`true` became 100). Zero and negative prices are still accepted;
  - places each entry by its timestamp. A bare wall-clock timestamp in the repeated hour resolves to the occurrence that continues the sequence. A bare wall-clock time the server time zone skips (02:00 on the spring-forward day) is dropped with a warning: JavaScript would move it onto 03:00 and the real 03:00 entry would then look like a duplicate and end the feed. Timestamps with an explicit offset, which GE-Spot uses, do not depend on the server time zone;
  - fills the missing repeated hour with the merged entry's price when the feed is contiguous in wall-clock time across the fall-back change. GE-Spot's 02:00 average covers both occurrences. GE-Spot stamps the merged entry with the first occurrence's offset (`+02:00`). A feed that stamps it with the second (`+01:00`) leaves the first occurrence open, and that hole now gets the merged price too, not the 01:00 price; the server time zone tells the two apart when it agrees with the feed's offsets. The spring-forward day (23 entries) was already contiguous and stays so;
  - ends the contiguous run at an entry with an invalid price: the feed listed that interval but gave no usable price for it, so it is never bridged with a made-up price. An entry whose timestamp cannot be parsed is dropped, and then no hole in that feed is filled, since the dropped entry may belong there;
  - bridges one omitted interval per run (one dropped hour, which GE-Spot documents for incomplete source data) by carrying the previous price forward, with a warning. The horizon stays full and there is no hour without a plan. A second omitted interval in the same run splits it like a longer gap;
  - splits the feed at any longer gap, and ends it at a duplicate or overlapping timestamp. A defective feed yields the contiguous run that covers the current slot, cut at the next gap, so the horizon gets shorter instead of the prices moving. If no run covers now, the previous prices are kept. Both cases log a warning. With two or more consecutive hours missing, the hours before the gap plan on a shrinking horizon and the gap itself has no plan (DESS keeps the last schedule) until a run covers now again;
  - keeps the previous prices when an otherwise clean feed starts after the current slot (for example a sensor that has already rolled over to the next day), instead of storing a series the planner would refuse;
  - sorts the entries by time when every timestamp carries an explicit offset, as GE-Spot's do. This is lossless, since each entry's instant is exact, so an out-of-order explicit-offset feed is accepted rather than ended. For bare wall-clock timestamps feed order stays authoritative, since it is what resolves the repeated hour, and an out-of-order entry ends the feed.
- **Load and price series that start after the plan window are refused** (port of upstream 8cb3a29). `extractWindow` zero-pads slots before a series starts. For load or prices that meant 0 W or 0 c/kWh in the first slots of the plan, which are the slots written to DESS first. This could happen after a `POST /data` injection, with a sensor whose list starts later, or with a timestamp parsed in the wrong zone. `buildSolverConfigFromSettings` now throws 422 `Series '<name>' starts after the plan window begins` for `load`, `importPrice` and `exportPrice`, with `now` and `seriesStart` in the details. `POST /calculate` returns that 422, and an auto-calculate cycle logs it and writes nothing, so DESS keeps the last schedule. PV and `evLoad` stay exempt, since leading zeros are legitimate for them. A fully stale series still reports `Insufficient future data` first. With `updateData: false`, auto-calculate now refreshes and retries once on either error. Production does not trigger the guard today: load starts at the run slot and HA prices at local midnight.
- **The hourly flows chart no longer inflates the first hour.** The hourly aggregation averaged power over the slots present in a bucket. A plan that starts at :15, :30 or :45 has a partial first hour, so its bar, tooltip kWh and negative-price injection kWh and cost were overstated by up to 4x (2x at :30). Energy flows are now divided by the number of slots in a full bucket, so absent slots count as zero. Prices are still averaged over the slots present. The flows and Load/PV charts now agree on the first bar. This is display only.
- **The Load/PV chart keeps both 02:00 hours on the fall-back night.** It bucketed by the local wall-clock hour, which folded the second 02:00 (CET) into the first. The result was one double-height bar and one hour missing from the axis. It now buckets by absolute hour like the flows aggregator, so "02:00" shows twice that night, as it should. This is display only.

## 0.7.61 - 2026-10-06

Hardware-safety release: the MQTT link to the GX recovers on its own, PV curtailment can no longer leave PV switched off after a restart, and the add-on shuts down cleanly. Nothing changes in how plans are computed, except that prediction sensors declared in `MWh` now count (see below).

- **The MQTT client keeps reconnecting after the GX refuses a login.** Since mqtt 5.16.0 (0.7.57), a refused CONNACK ("Not authorized") turns off auto-reconnect unless `reconnectOnConnackError` is set. That refusal is expected while a rebooting Cerbo has not loaded its security profile yet, during the broker's login rate limit, or after a password change. OptiVolt kept the dead client cached for good. Publishes and subscribes sat in its offline queue and never settled, and the first timed MQTT read then crashed the process through an unhandled rejection (`Timeout after 5000ms waiting for N/…/Soc`). The same crash also happened on any plain outage, on every mqtt version. Now:
  - the client sets `reconnectOnConnackError: true`;
  - every publish and subscribe has a 10 s deadline, after which the stalled client is dropped together with its offline queue, so stale setpoints are never sent late, and the next call reconnects;
  - reads settle at their own timeout even while the subscribe is stalled, with no orphaned rejection, and unsubscribes are fire-and-forget;
  - long-lived subscriptions (the shore optimizer's readings) move to the replacement client.

  Covered by a new socket-level suite (`tests/lib/victron-mqtt.broker.test.js`, real mqtt.js against a fake broker) that fails on 0.7.60. After deploying, check once: a Cerbo reboot should log `Connection refused…`, then a reconnect, then the next tick writing the schedule. A stall is logged once per dropped client, not once per queued publish (a schedule write queues hundreds).
- **`/health` returns 503 when work is genuinely stuck**: when an auto-calculate tick has been running for more than two intervals, or a plan/write run has been pending for more than 10 minutes. It never reports 503 just because Venus, VRM or HA is unreachable. The response only has an effect if the add-on's Watchdog is switched on.
- **A PV disable survives a crash only long enough to be undone.** PV curtailment only knew in memory that it had written `acsystem/<n>/Pv/Disable=1`. A SIGKILL, crash, OOM or power loss while curtailing, or a stop while Venus was unreachable, left PV (and the Enphase switch) off until the next negative-price window happened to toggle it again. Now:
  - before a live disable, the target (serial, acsystem instance, Enphase entity) is recorded in `DATA_DIR/pv-curtailment-state.json`. If that write fails, PV is not disabled. The record is fsynced (file, then directory) before the disable goes out, so a power loss right after it cannot leave an empty file. A record that exists but cannot be read or validated is still treated as a disable and restored with the target from the current settings, since `Pv/Disable=0` is the safe direction;
  - the record is deleted only after the restore is confirmed: `Pv/Disable` reads back 0 and the Enphase `turn_on` succeeded. Until then the restore is retried every tick, or every 30 s while the feature is off. That includes switching the feature off in settings while Venus is unreachable: the stop's restore fails, and the 30 s retry now takes over. A switch that is back on is not turned on again by later retries. Two halves that can fail forever without PV being off are given up, with an error in the log, after 20 failed attempts that span at least 9.5 minutes. Restores also run on every tick and plan update, so a short `tickMs` reaches 20 attempts sooner, but it cannot give up during a brief HA restart. The two halves are an Enphase switch call that keeps failing (entity renamed, HA down) and a read-back that never answers after a successful `Pv/Disable=0` (a stale serial or instance in the record). A read-back that answers 1 is never given up on;
  - at boot, a leftover record is restored with the recorded target, even if the feature is now off or in dry-run. No plan exists yet at boot, so this restores even inside a negative-price window, and the first plan disables PV again: the same off/on/off a clean restart does, since shutdown restores PV too. Holding the restore until the first plan was considered and rejected: a crash loop (for example one in the planning run itself) would then keep PV off indefinitely;
  - in dry-run, a leftover real disable is restored for real before anything is simulated, and retried every tick until confirmed. It can no longer be parked behind a simulated disable for the rest of a negative-price window;
  - a stop that lands while `Pv/Disable=1` is being published leaves the outcome to the stop's restore: the late disable no longer switches the Enphase array off or marks itself as owned;
  - without a record, a `Pv/Disable` that reads 1 at boot is not touched (single owner). It is logged as a warning and flagged as `externalDisable` in the service status, and the flag clears once it reads 0 again (re-checked each tick). This detection only runs while PV curtailment is enabled: with it off, the add-on does not open a Venus connection just to probe it. If a negative-price window starts while such an external disable is in place, curtailment takes the disable over as before, because it also has to switch the Enphase array off, and restores PV when the window ends. Disable PV on the GX again afterwards if it was meant to stay off.

  **Upgrading from 0.7.60:** a `Pv/Disable=1` that 0.7.60 failed to restore during the upgrade stop has no record, so 0.7.61 only reports it (`externalDisable`) and does not restore it. Upgrade outside a negative-price curtailment window, or check the add-on log after the upgrade for `Pv/Disable is 1 but OptiVolt holds no record of disabling it` and re-enable PV on the GX if needed.
- **Graceful shutdown that actually reaches Venus.** On SIGTERM the handler stops all timers, restores PV (bounded at 2 s), then closes the MQTT client (bounded at 0.4 s, force-ended on timeout), and exits. The force-end also cuts short a graceful close that is already waiting on its socket. mqtt.js ignores `end(true)` once a client is disconnecting, so that socket is destroyed instead. Before, it exited straight after the restore publish. mqtt.js was still holding that QoS-0 write in a corked socket, so the `Pv/Disable=0` was dropped: in 4 of 4 idle stops in the 0.7.60 image when no Enphase call followed it. Repeated signals share one shutdown, and a hard stop at 2.5 s keeps everything inside s6's 3 s grace time. Stopping PV curtailment now also detaches its config, so a plan finishing during shutdown cannot disable PV again.
- **The add-on runs `node api/index.ts` directly; tsx is gone.** tsx forwarded SIGTERM and then SIGKILLed node 30–60 ms later if the event loop was busy (a solve, a strategy-scoring loop). In that case none of the shutdown above ran. Changes:

  - The image build runs the same check, so a base-image change fails CI instead of the add-on.
  - Dropping tsx also removes the build-host esbuild binary the `deps` stage could copy into the aarch64 image, and about 80 MB of RSS.
  - The image now runs `apk upgrade`, so base packages such as OpenSSL and ca-certificates are current (OpenSSL 3.5.7 → 3.5.9 at the time of writing).
- **s6 finish script fixed.** The old v2 `s6-svscanctl -t /var/run/s6/services` line failed under s6-overlay v3 (exit 111). The script now only logs how the service ended, and s6 restarts it in place as before. Restart-in-place is deliberate: `config.yaml` has no watchdog.
- **Battery charge limiter: leaving live control releases the limit.** When the controller stops writing live, either because it is disabled or because it is switched to dry-run while staying enabled, it writes the top current rung once. Before, a reduced or 0 A limit stayed in place with nothing to step it back up. It only does this when it knows the register sits below the top rung, from its own command or from the seed it read while in control (which may be a limit an earlier OptiVolt process wrote before a restart). A controller that never got a reading writes nothing blind. Dry-run never wrote anything, so turning off a dry-run controller writes nothing either.

Watch the first auto-calculate plans and the adaptive-learning panel.

- **Auto-select runs that throw are now recorded.** An expired HA token, a WebSocket timeout or an unavailable recorder used to leave no trace except a console line, so the card kept showing the last *successful* outcome forever. - **Never write a non-optimal solve to the inverter.** An infeasible or unbounded solve produces all-zero rows, and `planAndMaybeWrite` pushed them to Victron as a real DESS schedule — commanding idle strategy and a 0% SoC target across every slot. Exposed as a "Charger phases" selector in the EV Charging settings card. **Default is three-phase** (the target hardware); single-phase deployments must set `evChargePhases = 1`, and callers that omit the field fall back to single-phase.
- **Chore:** Bump `vitest` and `@vitest/coverage-v8` to 4.1.8 (test tooling only).

## 0.7.20 - 2026-05-07

- Split inverter conversion losses out of the lumped charge/discharge efficiency. The LP previously applied `chargeEfficiency_percent` identically to `pv_to_battery` (DC→DC, no inverter) and `grid_to_battery` (AC→DC→battery), and applied **no** loss factor to `pv_to_grid` (DC→AC export). With prices like 26 c€ export now / 25.8 c€ import later, that math booked a tiny arbitrage profit for round-tripping solar through the grid — physically a loss because each AC↔DC crossing eats ~5%. New model:
  - New setting `inverterEfficiency_percent` (default 95) applied to every AC↔DC crossing: `pv_to_grid`, `pv_to_load`, `pv_to_ev`, `battery_to_load`, `battery_to_grid`, `battery_to_ev` (all DC→AC) and `grid_to_battery` (AC→DC).
  - `chargeEfficiency_percent` and `dischargeEfficiency_percent` now mean **battery only** (DC→stored and stored→DC bus).
  - LP flow variables now carry their natural source unit: `pv_to_*` are DC W from the PV bus, `grid_to_*` are AC W from grid, `battery_to_*` are DC W on the battery bus. AC↔DC factors are explicit in every constraint. ASCII diagram at the top of `lib/build-lp.ts` documents the conventions.
  - `parse-solution.ts` applies η_inv at the LP→PlanRow boundary so downstream consumers (UI, plan summary, DESS mapper, plan accuracy) keep reading AC-meter-equivalent values.
  - `dess-mapper.ts` saturation checks now compare DC battery flows against the DC `maxChargePower_W` / `maxDischargePower_W` correctly (previously mixed AC and DC).
  - Auto-split migration: existing `settings.json` files lacking `inverterEfficiency_percent` get a one-time migration. The legacy single combined value is split as `inverter = sqrt(max(legacy))`, with battery factors rescaled so the combined grid→battery / battery-export round-trip approximately matches old behavior. Default 95/95 → 97/97/97.
  - New setting appears in the UI immediately above charge/discharge efficiency on the System tab; also available in optimizer quick settings.
  - Added regression test for the user's exact scenario (PV at 26 c€, import at 25.8 c€): solver now correctly prefers `pv→battery` direct over the round trip when the spread is below the new ~10% physical round-trip loss threshold.
  - 15 new unit tests covering the η_inv physics on every flow path. All 1430 tests pass.
